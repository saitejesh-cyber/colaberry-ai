# Colaberry.ai UX Checklist (Laws of UX)

Use this checklist when reviewing pages, components, or new features.

## 1) Hierarchy & Von Restorff (distinctiveness)
- Primary message is the largest, most legible element on the page.
- Primary CTA is visually dominant and separated from secondary actions.
- One section per screen has a clear “headline + explanation + next action.”

## 2) Hick’s Law (reduce choices)
- Hero and top sections offer no more than 1–2 primary actions.
- Navigation limits choices and uses progressive disclosure for depth.
- Filters/tags are grouped and collapsible if many.

## 3) Fitts’s Law (target size)
- CTAs and cards have generous padding and are easily clickable.
- Icons and inputs are at least 40px tall on desktop and mobile.

## 4) Jakob’s Law (familiar patterns)
- Product layout follows familiar SaaS patterns (hero → proof → catalog → CTA).
- Search, filters, and catalogs behave like other AI/SaaS hubs.

## 5) Miller’s Law (chunking)
- Lists are grouped into 5–7 items per section.
- Long content is split into cards, tabs, or accordions.

## 6) Proximity & Similarity (grouping)
- Related items are grouped with shared visual styling.
- Spacing clearly separates unrelated sections.

## 7) Aesthetic–Usability
- Cards are consistent with one shadow, one border style, and clear typography.
- Color contrast is strong enough for quick scanning.

## 8) Postel’s Law (inputs)
- Search accepts partial queries; no “exact match required.”
- Tags/filters are forgiving and provide suggestions.

## 9) Peak–End Rule
- Strong first impression (hero) and strong last CTA (footer/close).

## 10) Doherty Threshold (responsiveness)
- Avoid heavy blur/filters that reduce scroll performance.
- Keep animations subtle and short (<300ms).

# The Design of Everyday Things (Don Norman) Addendum

## A) Affordances + Signifiers
- Buttons, cards, and links look obviously interactive.
- Primary actions use strong contrast and clear labels.

## B) Feedback
- Hover, focus, and active states are visible on all CTAs and cards.
- Loading/success states are communicated immediately.

## C) Constraints
- Reduce choices per section; limit competing CTAs.
- Use progressive disclosure for advanced options.

## D) Mapping
- Navigation matches user mental models (Catalog → Filters → Detail → Action).
- Headings and section order reflect the expected journey.

## E) Visibility
- Next best action is always visible above the fold.
- Critical paths (search, explore, request demo) are obvious.

## F) Error Prevention
- Avoid ambiguous labels (“Explore” → “Explore agents”).
- Provide hints, examples, and defaults in search/filters.
